# Predicting CYP1A2/2C9/2D6/3A4 Inhibition and Time-Dependent Inactivation: State of the Art and a Method Shortlist for the OpenADMET CYP Blind Challenge

## Bottom line

Three findings dominate everything else in this review. First, the CYP inhibition literature is almost entirely a *classification* literature built on one 2009 quantitative-HTS campaign, so the published AUC/MCC numbers — impressive as they look — say very little about how well anyone can regress a pIC50 inside a tight congeneric series, which is exactly what this challenge asks. Second, the prior OpenADMET challenges converged on a single winning recipe: a multitask Chemprop D-MPNN, pretrained (usually on CheMeleon), fed extra descriptors, ensembled with gradient-boosted trees, validated on a split that mimics the real test-set shift. The organizers' own attempt to improve on that pretraining recipe failed, which means the recipe is a floor to match cheaply rather than a frontier to push. Third, and most consequentially, in the PXR challenge the top 28 activity entries were statistically indistinguishable from one another, so the marginal return on model sophistication is close to zero once you are in the pack, and the leverage sits instead in data curation, label construction, and the two places where the pack will be thin: the TDI classification track and the ST-RAE metric's tolerance for uncertainty-aware predictions.

## Direct inhibition: what the field actually established

### The AID 1851 benchmark lineage and its ceiling

Nearly every published "CYP inhibition model" traces to a single source. Veith and colleagues at the NIH Chemical Genomics Center ran quantitative HTS against five recombinant CYP isozymes (1A2, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 3A4) across roughly 17,000 compounds, deposited as PubChem AID 1851 ([Veith 2009](https://doi.org/10.1038/nbt.1581)). That assay used luminogenic P450-Glo probes on recombinant enzyme, architecturally close to the challenge's fluorogenic DBOMF/EOMCC readouts, which makes it the most mechanistically relevant public prior we have.

The modeling lineage built on it is long and its trajectory is instructive. Performance rose quickly, then plateaued for a decade. Early combined-classifier work reached validation AUCs of 0.75–0.89 depending on isoform, with CYP3A4 and CYP1A2 consistently the hardest ([Cheng 2011](https://doi.org/10.1021/ci200028n)); interpretable rule-based C5.0 models and the WhichCyp SVM followed in the same range ([Su 2015](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.5b00130); [Rostkowski 2013](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btt325)). A multitask deep autoencoder over >13,000 PubChem compounds reported 86.4% cross-validated and 88.7% external accuracy across the five isoforms ([Li 2018](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.molpharmaceut.8b00110)). The direct answer to it is the single most useful methodological datapoint in this literature: benchmarking five ensemble and deep methods on the same task, XGBoost reached 90.4% average external accuracy and *beat* the multitask autoencoder's 88.5%, with the authors stating plainly that ensemble learning generally outperformed deep learning on the external test sets ([Wu 2019](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.9b00801)). That result has replicated: a systematic sweep over algorithms, representations, and splits for CYP3A4/2D6/2C9 found XGBoost and CatBoost on combined fingerprint-plus-descriptor features at AUC 0.92, with deep models averaging 0.89 on the same test sets ([Gong 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/jat.4601)).

Graph and transformer architectures have since posted higher numbers — multitask FP-GNN at average AUC 0.905, F1 0.779, MCC 0.647 ([Ai 2023](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2023.1099093)); a substructure-aware DNN at AUC 0.94–0.97 across the five isoforms on 85,715 compounds ([Chen 2023](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.3c01396)); a graph transformer with attention pooling ([Zonyfar 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13321-024-00915-z)); and a PCA-plus-SMOTE deep network that also separates strong from moderate inhibitors ([Xiao 2025](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.5c01192)). These are not directly comparable to each other, since each redefines the actives threshold, the split, and the deduplication policy, and a recent review of graph-based CYP ADMET models names exactly this dataset variability and generalization to novel chemical space as the field's open problems ([Abdelwahab 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12938-025-01412-6)).

The important limitation is structural rather than incremental. This entire body of work predicts a binary inhibitor/non-inhibitor label at a fixed threshold, and the challenge requires continuous pIC50 with credible intervals. Only CYPlebrity moves toward the harder problem by deliberately assembling public *and* proprietary data to fix the class-imbalance pathology, achieving inhibitor-to-non-inhibitor ratios from 1:1 to 1:3 and external MCCs of 0.62–0.70 ([Plonka 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bmc.2021.116388)), a useful calibration point for what "good" MCC looks like on CYP data, and notably close to the 0.647 of the best multitask GNN.

### Multitask learning across isoforms is the one consistently reproduced gain

Whether multitask learning helps is settled in the affirmative, and the mechanism is specific: it transfers signal from data-rich to data-poor isoforms. The cleanest demonstration handles CYP2B6 and CYP2C8, which have too little data to model alone, by borrowing from five better-covered isoforms across 12,369 compounds; multitask graph-convolutional models with imputation of missing labels significantly outperformed single-task equivalents ([Permadi 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13321-025-01015-2)). The imputation detail matters directly for our situation, because the announced training matrix is sparse across isoforms while the test matrix is dense across all four, which is the exact asymmetry that paper addresses. The original multitask autoencoder work went further and quantified *when* the gain appears, regressing the single-task-to-multitask difference on the properties of the auxiliary tasks ([Li 2018](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.molpharmaceut.8b00110)). The practical lesson, echoed by task-affinity grouping in the ExpansionRx results below, is that related tasks must be grouped deliberately rather than dumped into one head.

CYP-specific web platforms (SuperCYPsPred, ADMETlab 3.0, DEEPCYPs) package these models for inference ([Banerjee 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkaa166); [Fu 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkae236); [Ai 2023](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2023.1099093)), and reviews of the computational CYP field give useful orientation on where structure-based methods add value ([Kato 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dmpk.2019.11.006); [Tyzack 2019](https://doi.org/10.1111/cbdd.13445)). They are worth running as a sanity baseline, but the zero-shot evidence below argues strongly against trusting them on new chemistry.

### Molecular foundation models: real but narrow gains

The five model families named in our brief differ enormously in how well their claims survive contact with practical benchmarks.

CheMeleon is the one with a direct, quantified claim on the tasks we care about, and it deserves primacy because the challenge organizers use it themselves. It pretrains a Chemprop D-MPNN to regress Mordred descriptors for one million PubChem molecules, deliberately choosing low-noise computed targets over noisy experimental data or biased QM simulation, and on 58 Polaris and MoleculeACE benchmarks achieves a 75% win rate on Polaris tasks against Random Forest at 68%, fastprop at 36%, and vanilla Chemprop at 32%, plus a 97% win rate on MoleculeACE ([Burns 2025](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-8834086/v1)). The MoleculeACE number is the interesting one for us, because that benchmark is built specifically from activity cliffs, which is what a congeneric chemisimilar series generates.

The chemical language models are weaker on this evidence. ChemBERTa established SMILES-based masked-language pretraining ([Chithrananda 2020](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2010.09885)) and ChemBERTa-2 scaled it to 77M PubChem compounds, reporting only that it is "competitive with" existing architectures ([Ahmad 2022](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2209.01712)), a notably modest claim for that much compute. MoLFormer, trained on ~1.1 billion molecules with rotary embeddings and linear attention, makes the stronger case that a language model can recover spatial structure from strings alone ([Ross 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-022-00580-7)). Uni-Mol adds explicit 3D pretraining over 209M conformations ([Zhou 2022](https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv-2022-jjm0j-v3)), and MolE uses disentangled attention over graph structure with a two-step pretrain-then-multitask-finetune protocol ([Méndez-Lucio 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-53751-y)). GROVER's self-supervised graph transformer is the architectural ancestor of much of this ([Rong 2020](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2007.02835)), and MiniMol shows that 10M parameters pretrained on ~3,300 quantum and biological tasks can beat far larger models on TDC ADMET ([Kläser 2024](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2404.14986)), which matters because OpenADMET independently found MiniMol embeddings to be one of only two pretraining featurizations that reduced error.

Against these claims sits a body of skeptical work that any honest entry should internalize. A systematic study of what actually drives molecular property prediction found representation choice mattering less than dataset and split construction ([Deng 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-41948-6)); a careful comparison of descriptor-based against graph-based models found no general graph advantage ([Jiang 2021](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13321-020-00479-8)); and the MoleculeACE analysis showed that deep models systematically fail on activity cliffs where classical descriptor methods hold up ([van Tilborg 2022](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.2c01073)). This is the oldest lesson in the field, predating the foundation-model era. On a ChEMBL bioactivity benchmark with temporal validation, deep networks did beat established methods, but the margin depended heavily on the validation protocol chosen ([Lenselink 2017](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13321-017-0232-0)).

### Generalization to new chemical series: the actual task

Everything above is measured on random or scaffold splits. The challenge test set is 75 potent parents each surrounded by their 10 nearest ECFP4 neighbours from Enamine's in-stock catalogue, with the live/blind split drawn *between* series. That is a within-series interpolation problem nested inside a between-series extrapolation problem, and nobody has published a CYP-specific evaluation under that design.

The nearest evidence is a set of negative results, of which OpenADMET's own zero-shot audit is the sharpest. On ExpansionRx endpoints, ADMETlab 3.0 predicted LogD with R² = 0.02 and MAE = 0.82 against their internally trained model's R² = 0.50 and MAE = 0.57, while for Caco-2 permeability every zero-shot model tested — including OpenADMET's own — produced negative R². The stated reason is chemical-space distance. Average nearest-neighbour similarity between the ExpansionRx test set and ChEMBL was below 0.4, and below 0.3 for the curated TDC set. For reference on what those numbers mean, the 95% noise level for 2048-bit Morgan2 fingerprints is about 0.27. The corollary for us is that ChEMBL CYP data will not transfer as a finished model; it can only serve as pretraining or auxiliary-task signal.

Two more constraints deserve naming because they bound how good any model can be. The ST-RAE metric exists because the organizers know their own low-activity measurements are unreliable, and their in-house CYP3A4 pIC50 model trained on ~4,800 ChEMBL compounds reached MAE 0.42 and R² 0.52 with median predicted uncertainty of 1.5 pIC50 units, roughly five times the ~0.3 log-unit experimental error they cite as typical. Second, CYP3A4 inhibition is probe-dependent. Fourteen drugs assayed against multiple CYP3A4 probe substrates gave systematically different inhibition profiles ([Racha 2003](https://doi.org/10.2133/dmpk.18.128)), principal-component analysis of probe/inhibitor panels confirms the substrate-dependence structurally ([Nath 2008](https://doi.org/10.1124/dmd.108.022061)), and the mechanism has now been traced to allosteric effects at a peripheral site ([Wei 2024](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.3c01837)). ChEMBL CYP3A4 IC50 values aggregate midazolam, testosterone, and fluorogenic-probe assays indiscriminately, so a naive pooled training set injects probe-dependent variance the challenge's single-probe test set does not contain.

**What this implies for our entry.** Build the regression model as a four-task multitask D-MPNN with descriptor concatenation and CheMeleon initialization, not because graphs are inherently better but because it is the configuration the organizers' own benchmarking and two prior challenges independently converged on. Pair it with an XGBoost/LightGBM descriptor model and ensemble, since the classical baseline beat deep learning in the two most careful CYP-specific head-to-heads ([Wu 2019](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.9b00801); [Gong 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/jat.4601)). Curate ChEMBL CYP data by probe substrate and assay type rather than pooling it, and use it for pretraining or as auxiliary tasks rather than as a trusted label source. Validate on a leave-one-series-out split constructed to mimic the ECFP4 hit-expansion design; expect internal metrics to overstate leaderboard performance, as ExpansionRx participants reported.

## Time-dependent inhibition: a thinner literature and the real opportunity

### The label is an assay artifact as much as a molecular property

Understanding what `is_TDI` means requires understanding the IC50-shift assay that generates it, because the label's noise structure is inherited from assay design choices. Time-dependent inhibition is an umbrella term covering both mechanism-based inactivation and the formation of inhibitory metabolites, and mechanism-based inactivation is only *inferred* from an in vitro shift rather than demonstrated by it ([Riley 2007](https://doi.org/10.1517/17425255.3.1.51)). The shift is measured by pre-incubating compound with enzyme and NADPH before adding probe, and comparing the resulting IC50 to the no-pre-incubation arm, which is exactly the two-arm design in the challenge training pack.

Thresholds in the literature bracket the challenge's 2-fold cutoff. An IC50 fold-shift greater than 1.5 was proposed as indicating significant TDI, with the shifted IC50 recovering K_I and k_inact/K_I to within about 2-fold in most cases ([Berry 2008](https://doi.org/10.2174/187231208783478407)); two-time-point (10 and 30 min) shift designs were validated across six isoform/probe/inhibitor systems, showing that fast inactivators such as azamulin/CYP3A4 shift equally at both times while slow ones such as verapamil grow between them ([Perloff 2009](https://doi.org/10.1080/00498250802638155)). The challenge's 2-fold (0.301 log unit) threshold therefore sits at the conservative end of accepted practice, which should suppress false positives at the cost of missing slow inactivators.

The assay's known failure modes matter for label noise. The IC50-shift readout is confounded by depletion of active enzyme above ~1 μM and by inhibitory-metabolite formation masquerading as inactivation; a direct comparison across 24 structurally related compounds found percent-inhibition in a single-concentration loss-of-activity format correlating with k_inact/K_I at R² = 0.90 while fold-shift in IC50 managed only R² = 0.17, and concluded the shift assay is biased toward over-predicting in vivo interaction and more likely to yield false positives ([Wong 2016](https://doi.org/10.3109/00498254.2016.1143139)). A refined two-pair-of-curves protocol was developed specifically to separate true time-dependence from metabolite-mediated reversible inhibition ([Li 2011](https://doi.org/10.1124/dmd.111.038208)), and the broader methodological critiques are worth reading before trusting any shift-derived label ([Stresser 2013](https://doi.org/10.1517/17425255.2014.856882); [Yan 2012](https://doi.org/10.2174/156802612800672871)).

Base rates set expectations for class balance. Screening 400 registered drugs with a single-concentration k_obs assay and an empirical positive threshold of 0.02 min⁻¹ flagged only 4% as positive, rising above 20% for in-house lead-optimization compounds ([Zimmerlin 2011](https://doi.org/10.1124/dmd.110.037911)). Since the challenge library is Enamine diversity plus FDA-approved compounds and positives are called by shift rather than k_obs, a positive rate somewhere in the single-digit-to-low-tens percent is the reasonable prior. That is severe imbalance, which is precisely why MCC was chosen. Related work established k_inact/K_I cutoffs of 1.1 (FDA) versus 1.25 (EMA draft) for triggering clinical DDI studies, finding the FDA value prone to false positives ([Kosaka 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xphs.2017.04.077)), and reviews cover risk assessment and early decision-making frameworks ([Fujioka 2012](https://doi.org/10.1124/dmd.112.046649); [Grime 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejps.2008.10.002); [Riley 2015](https://doi.org/10.1517/17425255.2015.1013095)).

### Structural alerts for reactive metabolites: chemistry we can encode directly

Unlike direct inhibition, TDI has genuine mechanistic substructure rules, and they are well enough characterized to write as SMARTS. The canonical compilation of structural alerts, with explicit medicinal-chemistry guidance on which to avoid and which are tolerable, remains the reference ([Stepan 2011](https://doi.org/10.1021/tx200168d)), updated with practical strategies for designing around alerts ([Kalgutkar 2019](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jmedchem.9b00917)) and organized systematically for computational use ([Claesson 2018](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemrestox.8b00046)).

Alert classes map to distinct mechanisms, which is why a single "reactive" flag underperforms a per-class featurization. Terminal acetylenes are oxidized to ketene or oxirene species that alkylate the heme or apoprotein, the classic suicide-substrate route ([Ortiz de Montellano 2019](https://doi.org/10.1080/03602532.2019.1632891)). Methylenedioxyphenyl/benzodioxole groups act differently: they generate carbene intermediates that form quasi-irreversible metabolic-intermediate complexes with the heme iron ([Murray 2000](https://doi.org/10.2174/1389200003339270)), a mechanism confirmed for piperine-type and stiripentol-type compounds against CYP3A and CYP1A2 respectively ([Masubuchi 2024](https://doi.org/10.1124/dmd.123.001511)). Thiophenes bioactivate via S-oxide or epoxide routes ([Gramec 2014](https://doi.org/10.1021/tx500134g)), and quantum-chemical work on furans, phenols, nitroaromatics, and thiophenes shows that alert reactivity is *context-dependent*, with the same ring is benign or dangerous depending on substitution ([Dang 2017](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemrestox.6b00336)). This is the strongest argument for learned rather than hand-coded alert features: a binary SMARTS match discards the substituent context that determines whether the alert fires. Broader reviews cover the alert-to-toxicity relationship and its limits ([Kalgutkar 2015](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-pharmtox-010814-124720)), computational treatments of inactivation mechanism ([Mirzaei 2021](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemrestox.0c00483)), and the MI-complex assay itself ([Russell 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/bs.mie.2023.08.002); [Takakusa 2011](https://doi.org/10.1124/dmd.110.037531); [Kamel 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ddtec.2012.09.011)).

Known inactivator sets exist for both target isoforms. For CYP3A4 the therapeutic-drug inactivators are catalogued in a standard review ([Zhou 2005](https://doi.org/10.2165/00003088-200544030-00005)), and the structure-activity analysis with mitigation strategies is the medicinal-chemist's version of the same list ([Orr 2012](https://doi.org/10.1021/jm300065h)). For CYP2D6 the mechanism-based inactivators are individually characterized rather than compiled — paroxetine, MDMA, and a series of natural products including notopterol, nitidine chloride, berberrubine and phellopterin — and this scarcity is itself informative. CYP2D6 TDI is rare, so the positive class in that column will be very small.

### Published TDI prediction models: only four, and one is decisive

The predictive-modeling literature here is remarkably thin, which is the opportunity. Pfizer built Bayesian classifiers on over 2,000 compounds from a 2+2 automated assay, validated at ROC > 0.847, and reported that indazole-containing substructures dominated the TDI-eliciting patterns ([Zientek 2010](https://doi.org/10.1021/tx900417f)). A literature-and-database-curated CYP3A4 TDI set was modeled with both classical ML and deep learning, with GraphConv on a balanced dataset performing best, external screening validated experimentally, and structural alerts extracted by information gain ([Xu 2022](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.molpharmaceut.2c00571)). Five QSAR models covering CYP3A4 TDI plus reversible inhibition of 3A4/2C9/2C19/2D6 were trained on 10,129 chemicals harvested from FDA approval packages and literature, reporting cross-validated sensitivity of 78–84% and up to 75% sensitivity on external validation, alongside a curated MBI structural-alert collection responsive to the 2020 FDA DDI guidance ([Faramarzi 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2024.1451164)).

The fourth is the one to build against. Novartis modeled CYP3A4 TDI, evaluated it prospectively, and, most usefully, compared model error to the variability of the experimental assay itself, using multitask learning to borrow from public data, in-house CYP assays, and other pharmaceutical companies via federated learning. Their three-class scheme (negative at k_obs < 0.01 min⁻¹, then weak and strong) reflects a considered judgment that binary TDI labels discard information ([Fluetsch 2024](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemrestox.3c00305)). Two things follow. The state of the art in TDI prediction is a multitask model that treats direct inhibition as an auxiliary task, which is exactly the architecture the challenge's dual-track structure invites. And the meaningful performance target is the assay's own reproducibility, not an arbitrary MCC.

**What this implies for our entry.** Treat TDI as a shift-regression problem with a threshold applied at inference, not as a native classification problem. The training pack supplies both arms, so the underlying quantity Δ = pIC50(+NADPH) − pIC50(direct) is continuous and learnable, and it retains the information that a 0.301-log threshold discards. Predict the two arms jointly with the four direct-inhibition endpoints in one multitask model, following [Fluetsch 2024](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemrestox.3c00305). Featurize alerts per mechanism class (acetylene, MDP/benzodioxole, thiophene, furan, aniline, hydrazine, thioamide) rather than as one aggregate flag, and keep substituent context ([Dang 2017](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemrestox.6b00336)). Tune the decision threshold explicitly to maximize MCC under the expected imbalance rather than defaulting to 0.5, and expect the CYP2D6 column to have very few positives. Also screen for fluorescence interference and frequent-hitter behaviour, since three of four assays are fluorogenic and known ML filters exist ([Stork 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/cmdc.201700673); [Stork 2019](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.8b00677)); the CYP2D6 endpoint uses label-free acoustic-ejection MS instead ([Liu 2022](https://doi.org/10.1080/17460441.2022.2084069)) and so has different, non-optical failure modes.

## What the prior OpenADMET challenges actually taught

### The ExpansionRx method census

The organizers tabulated model choices for the top 20 finishers of the ExpansionRx challenge (370+ participants, launched 27 October 2025, closed 19 January 2026), and the table is the most direct evidence available about what wins in this specific competition format. Their own summary headline is that multitask GNNs were the undisputed winner, with Chemprop the most popular single choice; KERMT and Uni-Mol2 also appeared, while TabPFN was popular but did not reach top performance.

Counting from that table: Chemprop or a Chemprop variant appears in roughly 15 of the top 20 entries, CheMeleon pretraining in 8, and multitask learning in 18 of 20. Three of the top 10 combined MPNNs with classical ML (Random Forest, LightGBM, XGBoost, CatBoost), which the organizers singled out as promising. Task-affinity grouping, placing related endpoints in complementary groups, was used by several top performers.

Four findings from that post-mortem are directly actionable. External data was near-universal among top finishers, and 4 of the top 5 used proprietary data; the organizers concluded this lends credence to a "more is more" narrative while noting that excellent performance was achieved without it. Hyperparameter optimization was largely ineffective, with most top entries reporting minimal gain and the organizers judging that data, feature engineering, and augmentation mattered more. Internal validation strategy was a critical differentiator, with top performers favouring temporal splits that mirrored the prospective task, and one entrant using difficulty-based splits that held out compounds the model found hard; multiple participants reported that internal cross-validation metrics did not correspond to leaderboard performance. And per-endpoint difficulty tracked data availability and additivity: LogD was predicted well because it is largely additive, whereas intrinsic clearance was hardest because it is sensitive to small structural changes. That is a warning for us, since CYP inhibition within a congeneric series is closer to clearance than to LogD in that respect.

### The PXR challenge and the tiering result

The PXR challenge (announced results 3 July 2026, activity track ranked by MAE) delivered the finding that should shape our expectations more than any other. Of 100 evaluated activity entries, **the top tier contained 28 entries that were not statistically distinguishable from the rank-1 submission**, spanning MAE 0.4061 to 0.4360, a spread of 0.03 log units. In the structure track, the top tier held 10 of 30 entries. The organizers moved from Holm-Bonferroni to Benjamini-Hochberg FDR control at 5% precisely because the conservative correction left tiers "overly crowded and ambiguous" across 4,950 pairwise comparisons.

The halfway-point post makes the arithmetic explicit: bootstrap standard deviation averaged 0.02 while the average difference between top-10 entries was 0.002, an order of magnitude smaller than the noise. Their statistical-methodology post reinforces it with a worked example, noting that claiming a winner on a 0.01 MAE difference is mathematically suspicious when assay uncertainty is around ±0.3 log units, and showing a case where the third-place entry beat the second-place entry in 211 of 1,000 bootstrap resamples.

The metric family is stable across challenges, which lets us anticipate its behaviour. ExpansionRx used MA-RAE, described as normalizing MAE to the dynamic range of the test data so that endpoints on different scales carry equal weight, which matters under endpoint imbalance. The CYP challenge extends this to MA-ST-RAE, where per-compound error is the distance from the prediction to the nearest bound of the ground-truth pIC50 credible interval and is zero anywhere inside that interval. Winning ExpansionRx MA-RAE was 0.5113 ± 0.0070, with the top 12 spanning 0.5113 to 0.5824.

### The organizers' own negative result on CheMeleon

The most strategically valuable post is the one reporting failure. OpenADMET, working with CheMeleon's originator, ran an extensive campaign to improve the model: six prescaling ablations, 15 pretraining featurization schemes, MolPILE subsets at 1M/5M/10M molecules, generative augmentation with ANNalog, five message-passing schemes including GINE and MPNN++, and LLM-driven autoresearch over the design space. Their conclusion was that no change improved performance across the board, and that the CheMeleon pretraining strategy is robust to hyperparameter and pretraining-methodology choices.

Specific results are worth carrying. Only two featurizations gave a net 6% MAE reduction: MiniMol embeddings and a Novartis surrogate-data model, the latter being ADMET endpoint predictions rather than computed descriptors. Increasing pretraining diversity *hurt*. MolPILE was a significant disadvantage, and clustering showed almost all MolPILE-only clusters were singletons against an average of 90 molecules for CheMeleon-only clusters. A narrow 40k generated set targeted at the challenge chemistry outperformed the 10M diverse set overall. Frozen MPNN weights performed worse than a 1e-4 fine-tuning rate. And the autoresearch run improved pretraining MSE by 36% while yielding under 10% fine-tuning gain, with pretraining performance a poor proxy for downstream performance. The post closes by pointing at tabular foundation models built on top of the CheMeleon fingerprint as the direction the literature suggests.

That pointer lines up with independent evidence. TabPFN performs in-context learning on small tabular problems without task-specific training ([Hollmann 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08328-6)), tabular foundation models over frozen molecular-foundation-model representations perform well in the low-to-medium data regime at reduced computational cost, and a drug-discovery-specific assessment finds TabPFN opens new avenues for small-data tabular learning ([Chen 2026](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.5c02823)). TabPFN was popular but not top-performing in ExpansionRx, though it was used there on raw descriptors rather than stacked on CheMeleon embeddings, which is the untested combination.

A related organizer post quantifies where proprietary data helps and where public data actively hurts. Using Novartis surrogate data — knowledge-distilled predictions over 274,000 compounds spanning 25 endpoints, including a four-task CYP inhibition model covering reversible *and* time-dependent inhibition — surrogate fine-tuning was best for Caco-2, lifting Spearman ρ from 0.34 to 0.70 and R² from −0.01 to 0.28 relative to ChEMBL extrapolation. For mouse plasma protein binding, by contrast, adding external pretraining *increased* MAE by more than 0.2 and ChEMBL fine-tuning drove R² below −1.2. External data is not free; it helps where the source assay matches and harms where it does not.

**What this implies for our entry.** Do not spend effort trying to beat CheMeleon's pretraining, because the organizers already tried comprehensively and failed. Spend it instead on the three places their own results point: a narrow, on-target pretraining or augmentation set built around the challenge's Enamine/FDA chemistry rather than a broader one; surrogate-style ADMET pseudo-labels as pretraining targets, which is one of only two featurizations that helped and for which a public CYP-inhibition surrogate model exists; and a tabular foundation model stacked on CheMeleon embeddings, which they explicitly name as the untried next step. Accept that the regression leaderboard will be a statistical tie and optimize for landing in the top tier cheaply, then differentiate on the TDI track, where the field is thin, and on the innovation award, which is explicitly decoupled from rank. Expect internal validation to mislead; build a leave-one-series-out protocol and trust it over cross-validation.

## Method shortlist

Eight approaches, ranked by expected value per unit effort. `method_shortlist.csv` carries the structured version with effort, GPU requirement, and reference DOIs.

1. **Multitask Chemprop v2 + CheMeleon, all six endpoints jointly.** Four direct pIC50 tasks plus both TDI-arm regressions in one model, descriptors concatenated, MPNN learning rate 1e-4. Proven by two prior challenges and by the organizers' own tooling; this is the floor rather than the ceiling. Low risk, moderate effort.
2. **Shift-regression TDI with threshold tuned for MCC.** Predict Δ = pIC50(+NADPH) − pIC50(direct) continuously, then threshold at 0.301 with the operating point chosen to maximize MCC. Follows [Fluetsch 2024](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemrestox.3c00305) in treating TDI as continuous and multitask. This is the highest-leverage item because the TDI literature is thin and MCC is unforgiving of a badly placed threshold.
3. **Gradient-boosted descriptor ensemble blended with the D-MPNN.** XGBoost/LightGBM/CatBoost on RDKit + Mordred + ECFP, blended with the graph model. Beat deep learning in the two most careful CYP head-to-heads ([Wu 2019](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.9b00801); [Gong 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/jat.4601)) and three of the ExpansionRx top 10 used hybrid ensembles. Cheap insurance.
4. **Probe-aware ChEMBL curation with assay-type stratification.** Split CYP3A4 records by probe substrate and assay technology before use, and pass provenance as a feature or separate task. Justified by documented probe-dependence ([Racha 2003](https://doi.org/10.2133/dmpk.18.128); [Nath 2008](https://doi.org/10.1124/dmd.108.022061); [Wei 2024](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.3c01837)) and by the organizers' finding that mismatched external data hurts. Unglamorous, high expected value.
5. **Mechanism-class structural-alert featurization.** Per-class SMARTS with substituent context for the seven principal reactive-metabolite classes, as features rather than rules. Grounded in the alert compilations ([Stepan 2011](https://doi.org/10.1021/tx200168d); [Claesson 2018](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemrestox.8b00046)) and in the context-dependence result ([Dang 2017](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemrestox.6b00336)). Interpretable and directly relevant to TDI.
6. **Interval-aware ST-RAE-matched loss with calibrated uncertainty.** Train against the metric: predict a distribution and place the point estimate to minimize expected distance to the credible-interval boundary. Conformal prediction gives the calibration machinery ([Norinder 2014](https://doi.org/10.1021/ci5001168)); the organizers' own uncertainty work reports median predicted uncertainty five times experimental error, so calibration is demonstrably unsolved here. Speculative but a strong innovation-award candidate, and no published work optimizes directly against ST-RAE.
7. **TabPFN stacked on frozen CheMeleon embeddings.** In-context learning over foundation-model representations, the combination the organizers explicitly name as untried ([Hollmann 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08328-6); [Chen 2026](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.5c02823)). Low effort, no training, genuinely novel in this context.
8. **On-target pretraining or augmentation over Enamine/FDA chemical space.** A narrow pretraining set built from the challenge's own libraries, optionally with surrogate ADMET pseudo-labels. Directly supported by the finding that a 40k on-target generated set beat a 10M diverse one and that surrogate pseudo-descriptors were one of two featurizations that helped. Moderate effort, moderate GPU.

Two families were deliberately excluded. 3D/conformer-based approaches such as Uni-Mol are out because the challenge assays recombinant enzyme with no structural component in the activity tracks and the E3FP result was among the worst featurizations tested. Heavy hyperparameter optimization is out because the ExpansionRx post-mortem found it ineffective.

## Open questions this review could not resolve

The exact ST-RAE normalization denominator is unpublished, so the trivial-predictor baseline that sets the scale is unknown. The credible-interval width and level for test-set pIC50 values are likewise unpublished, which prevents pre-computing how much slack approach 6 actually buys. No published CYP model has been evaluated under a hit-expansion, leave-one-series-out design, so the literature offers no direct estimate of achievable within-series MAE. And the expected TDI positive rate for this specific library is a prior extrapolated from a 400-registered-drug study ([Zimmerlin 2011](https://doi.org/10.1124/dmd.110.037911)) with a different threshold definition, not a measurement.
